# Life in Vietnam — 3D life-simulation design

- **Status:** design draft for approval
- **Date:** 2026-11-03
- **Repo:** `agent-arena`
- **Working title:** *Một Đời* (One Life)
- **Mode name:** Life in Vietnam

## 1. Intent

Build a 3D, first-person life simulation inside Agent Arena: the player is born in Vietnam, lives one complete life from birth to death, and experiences the turning points as places they can walk through and people they can talk to. The life itself — birthplace, family, health, school, work, love, children, migration, death — is generated by the **1in8b Engine** from real demographic statistics. The LLM does not invent the life; it directs each beat into a 3D scene. The player makes the choices the engine offers.

The 2D game is a dashboard with a timeline. The 3D version should feel like being inside that timeline: the room during the economic crisis, the classroom on the first day, the motorbike ride to the first job, the hospital corridor, the family altar at the end.

## 3. The experience

### 3.1 Start screen

- Country: **Vietnam** (fixed in v1; shown, not editable).
- Period: **Past** (year slider, 1986–2025), **Today** (2026), **Future** (projected world), **Any** (random). The v1 picker marks 1950–1985 as “later”; the first expansion adds that era.
- Sex: female, male, or random.
- Optional advanced conditions: family income fifth, city or village, faith, disability. Collapsed by default; the engine draws the rest.
- 1in8b API key field, stored locally, never shipped. A short “Get a free key” link opens the instructions (`api@1in8b.life`).
- Scene director model: reuse the app’s existing LLM model configuration, or “Template scenes only” (no LLM).
- Content notice: this is a simulation, not a real person; ages 15+; death, illness, and hardship appear; sensitive content is off by default.

### 3.2 Birth card

A short registration card, like the 2D game: name, sex, year, country, place (city/village and region when the engine provides it), parents’ ages and work, family income fifth, faith. It shows only fields the engine returns; country-level life expectancy, under-5 mortality, and GDP are not part of the `/live` response and are not promised in v1. The card is the first journal page. The 3D prologue opens in the birthplace: a delivery room or a home, the mother, the father, a grandparent, the sounds of the era. The player is the baby; agency is limited to looking, reaching, and crying.

### 3.3 A life in chapters

The run is a sequence of chapters. Each chapter has a target age, 1–3 staged scenes, a major decision or key event, and a “years pass” summary that fills the journal. Quiet years are not playable; they are read in the journal and shown as a short montage.

| Chapter | Target ages | Scenes | Typical beats |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Birth | 0 | 1 | birth card, first look, naming |
| Early childhood | 1–5 | 1–2 | first steps, first words, home life, temperament; reflection at 5 |
| School | 6–11 | 1–2 | first day, classroom, friends, first focus choice |
| Adolescence | 12–18 | 2 | schoolyard, exam or stream choice, first love, identity; reflection at 15 |
| Early adulthood | 19–29 | 2–3 | first job, city or migration, partner, marriage |
| Midlife | 30–49 | 2–3 | career, children, a parent’s death, health scare; reflection at 30 |
| Late life | 50–69 | 2 | retirement, grandchildren, illness; reflection at 50 |
| Elder | 70+ | 1 | reflection, family, the last room; reflection at 70 |
| Death | age at death | 1 | quiet deathbed, life review, memorial |

A life that ends early truncates the arc with dignity: fewer scenes, but still a birth, a life, and an ending. A life that migrates keeps the camera in Vietnam for v1; the abroad years are summarized in the journal, with a station or video-call scene if the director wants one.

### 3.4 Inside a scene

- First-person camera at the character’s height, which rises with age.
- WASD or arrow keys + mouse look on desktop; left joystick + drag look on touch.
- Interact with **E**, click, or tap on a highlighted person or object.
- People talk in short speech bubbles; the dialogue panel holds the current exchange.
- The engine’s decision is presented as 2–4 things the player can **do**, not as a numbered card. Each engine choice is bound to an interactable: sit at the desk, follow the friend outside, take the mother’s hand, open the letter.
- The year’s **focus** is chosen by using an object or place: the desk (study), the yard (play), the altar (family), the gym (health), the drawer (save money).
- At chapter boundaries, a light **spending** moment uses the engine’s sheet: school fees, medicine, a motorbike, home repairs. The player may buy zero to two items; purchases are sent as `buy` with the next step. This is a small panel, not a full scene, and counts as an economic choice.
- The player can also look, sit, open, and listen for flavor. Flavor interactions never change the engine state.
- “Let time pass” resolves the current beat with the engine’s default policy and moves on.
- The scene holds until the beat is resolved. There is no real-time clock and no fail state.

### 3.5 The journal

`Tab` or `J` opens a diegetic journal, the only dense-text surface in the game:

- the full engine record, newest first, including historical events and data notes;
- health, happiness, knowledge, bonds, income, net worth;
- family and household, with ages and work;
- the life map (one square per year) and survival curve;
- the place and era facts;
- the player’s written thoughts from the 5/15/30/50/70 pauses;
- the engine credit and the simulated-life disclaimer.

### 3.6 Reflections

At ages 5, 15, 30, 50, and 70 the game offers a quiet pause: a bench, a window, a bed, a bus stop. The player can write one thought. It stays in the journal and appears in the life review. This is the emotional spine of the 2D game and must survive the adaptation.

The reflection is a game-layer pause, not an engine decision. If an engine decision happens to sit at the same age, the scene resolves the decision first (or merges it into the same quiet moment) and then offers the thought. The player can skip the thought; it never changes the life.

### 3.7 Death and memorial

- The death scene is quiet: a room, a window, a hand, a fade. No medical detail, no graphic imagery.
- A life review follows: the staged scenes as stills, the journal’s key lines, the written thoughts, the family tree, the cause of death as the engine names it (or “died peacefully” when the engine gives no cause).
- A small memorial space: a cemetery or family altar with incense; the player can light a candle and read the life summary.
- The closing credit: “Life simulated by the 1in8b Engine (1in8b.life). Not a real person.” plus the source credits.
- “Live another life” returns to the start screen with a new seed.

## 4. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

### Goals

1. A complete life, birth to death, playable in one 45–75 minute session.
2. Every major engine decision is playable through 3D interaction, not through a text menu in a HUD; target 8+ meaningful choices without ever inventing one.
3. The life facts and odds come from the 1in8b Engine; the LLM never overrides them.
4. The journal contains the full engine record, so the summarized years are still present.
5. Vietnam feels specific: era, place, income, family, food, streets, school, weather.
6. The game works without an LLM key via a deterministic scene director.
7. The player’s 1in8b key never leaves their browser except to `1in8b.life`, and never reaches the LLM.

### Non-goals

- Every year playable, or a continuous open world.
- 172 countries in v1; Vietnam only.
- Photoreal graphics, full-body simulation, or a physics sandbox.
- Combat, war gameplay, or graphic death.
- Multiplayer, accounts, leaderboards, or monetization.
- Republishing the 1in8b site, its text, art, or UI.
- Presenting simulated lives as real people, statistics, or forecasts.
- Shipping a 1in8b API key inside the build.

### Anti-goals

- A slideshow of text with a 3D background.
- A game where the player’s choices are cosmetic and the engine decides everything.
- A game that invents life facts that contradict the engine.
- A game that depicts sensitive content graphically or gratuitously.
- A game that collapses under asset scope; modular kits only.
- A game that leaks the engine key or logs it.

## 5. Constraints

### 5.1 The 1in8b Engine

The game uses `POST https://1in8b.life/api/v1/live` as its authoritative life engine.

Start a life:

```json
{
  "country": "VNM",
  "sex": "female",
  "year": 1995,
  "fifth": "3",
  "place": "village",
  "policy": "auto",
  "sensitive": 0,
  "suicide": 0,
  "seed": 12345
}
```

`year` is 1950–2025; use `future: 1` instead for the projected world; omit both for today. Each step:

```json
{ "token": "...", "choose": "key", "focus": "key", "buy": ["id"], "until": "decision" }
```

The response carries `state`, `events`, `decision`, `focus`, `sheet`, `ended`, `death`, `token`, `meta`, and `usage`. Use `{ "token": "...", "view": true }` to look without advancing (no units). The token is sealed and valid for 90 days.

Other facts that shape the build:

- CORS is open (`Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`), so a BYO key can call from the browser.
- Free plan: 2,000 life units a month; a life is 1 unit (2 for a past or future birth); each step is 1 unit. 30 engine calls a minute.
- The response text is English; names and catalogs are multilingual (13 languages), but the record is English.
- `sensitive: 0` and `suicide: 0` keep the hardest lines out; the game will not ask for them.
- The engine version changes; store `meta.engine` with each run.

### 5.2 API terms that are product constraints

- Credit **“1in8b Engine (1in8b.life)”** wherever the life is shown, and say plainly that it is simulated and not a real person.
- Do not copy or rebuild the engine, its tables, or the 1in8b site’s text, art, or UI.
- Do not publish the engine’s answers as a dataset; do not train or tune a model on them.
- Do not offer the API as a service of your own; one key per player, kept private.
- Do not present simulated lives as real records, official statistics, or forecasts.
- Do not use who a person is (faith, group, caste, orientation, gender, disability) to rank or demean; show difference as the data has it.
- Handle hard lines with care: no method, no detail, no reward, no joke, never aimed at children, and help-seeking nearby.

### 5.3 App constraints

- Agent Arena is a client-only SPA: no backend, no server-side secret.
- The existing app already stores LLM keys in `localStorage` and calls providers directly; the 1in8b key follows the same pattern.
- The app ships 10 UI locales; Vietnamese is not one of them.
- The repo’s `src/lib` coverage threshold is 80% on all metrics; new game logic should be tested even where the threshold does not apply.
- Node 20+ (CI uses 24); lint, test, and build must pass.
- Existing assets are Kenney low-poly packs; new assets need attribution and `npm run assets:inventory`.

## 6. Architecture

### 6.1 Mode integration

Life in Vietnam is a new full-screen mode in `agent-arena`, not a separate app.

- A “Live a life” entry on the arena HUD/landing screen, plus `?life` for a direct link.
- When the mode is active, the arena canvas unmounts (or goes dormant) and `<LifeGame />` mounts.
- The mode is lazy-loaded with `React.lazy` / dynamic import so the arena’s initial bundle does not grow.
- `App.tsx` gets a small `lifeMode` branch; the arena’s behavior is otherwise untouched.

### 6.2 Modules

```
src/game/
  LifeGame.tsx                 orchestration + phase router
  engine/
    oneIn8b.ts                 typed /live client, errors, retries, usage
    types.ts                   response types + runtime guards
    sceneDirector.ts           LLM prompt, schema validation, fallback
    deterministicDirector.ts   template mapping without an LLM
  state/
    lifeStore.ts               Zustand: run, engine state, chapter, scene, journal
  scenes/
    templates/*.json           authored scene definitions
    dressing/*.ts              era/income/place overrides
    LifeScene.tsx              R3F scene renderer
    LifeCharacter.tsx          GLB character, age scaling, idle, speech
    LifeProps.tsx              placeable loading from the catalog
    sceneRegistry.ts           template + dressing resolver
  controls/
    FirstPersonControls.tsx    pointer lock, WASD, touch joystick
  ui/
    StartScreen.tsx
    BirthCard.tsx
    LifeHud.tsx
    Dialogue.tsx
    Journal.tsx
    PauseMenu.tsx
    DeathScreen.tsx
    Memorial.tsx
    LifeSettings.tsx
  audio/
    ambience.ts
  i18n.ts                      life-specific strings, merged into src/i18n
```

Reuse from the existing app:

- `src/lib/three/glbFit.ts`, `characterRig.ts` for character loading, height fitting, head/hand scaling.
- `src/lib/assets/catalog.ts` and the placeable GLBs for props.
- `src/lib/ai/providers.ts` (`chatCompletion`) for the scene director.
- `src/lib/config` and `prompts.yaml` patterns.
- `src/lib/maps/schema.ts` / `blankMap` / theme and floor surfaces for scene maps.
- `src/lib/storage.ts` patterns and `src/lib/dayNight.ts` lighting.
- `src/i18n` infrastructure.

Do not couple the life store to the arena store. The life mode has its own Zustand store and its own persistence key (`agent-arena-life`), with the API key in a separate key (`agent-arena-life-key`) so it can be cleared independently.

### 6.3 Run flow

```
Start screen
  -> POST /live (start) -> birth card
  -> chapter plan (target ages)
  -> view / advance to the next beat
     -> if the beat is a major decision or key event:
          scene director -> ScenePlan
          LifeScene renders the place
          player interacts -> chooses an engine choice
          POST /live { token, choose, focus?, buy? }
     -> if the beat is minor:
          choose the policy/default option, log it, continue
  -> time-skip summary between chapters
  -> ... until ended
  -> death scene -> life review -> memorial -> save completed life
```

The game never calls the engine while the player is standing still in a scene. Each network step is tied to a resolved beat. The UI shows a small “the world is deciding” transition if a call takes longer than ~400ms.

### 6.4 Engine client

`oneIn8b.ts` responsibilities:

- build start/step requests;
- attach `Authorization: Bearer <key>` only to 1in8b requests;
- validate required response fields and tolerate the API’s loose `additionalProperties`;
- map errors: `key` (401), `rate` (429), `month` (quota), `not_in_world` (400), `too_much_work`, `token`/`live_state`, `unavailable`;
- retry only idempotent reads and failed steps with the same token; never retry a step with a new token;
- expose `usage` for a settings panel;
- never log the key or the full token.

The key is read from `agent-arena-life-key` at call time. It is never put in the life store, never persisted in the run JSON, never sent to the LLM, and never shown in error messages.

The client sits behind a small transport interface so a hosted proxy can be added later without changing the game. v1 calls 1in8b directly with the player’s key.

### 6.5 Life store

Phases: `setup` → `birth` → `chapter` → `death` → `review` → `memorial`.

Run state:

```ts
interface LifeRun {
  id: string;
  startedAt: number;
  engineVersion: string;
  country: "VNM";
  config: { year?: number; future?: boolean; sex?: "female" | "male"; seed?: number; fifth?: string; place?: string; faith?: string };
  token: string;
  state: LifeState;          // name, age, year, job, income, health, ...
  chapterIndex: number;
  sceneIndex: number;
  journal: JournalEntry[];   // engine events + decisions + summaries + thoughts
  thoughts: Record<number, string>; // age -> written reflection
  completed: boolean;
}
```

Persist the run, journal, thoughts, and completed lives. The token is the only way to resume; if it expires, the game offers a summary and a new life. Never persist the key inside the run.

### 6.6 Scene director

Input: chapter, engine `state`, `events` since the last beat, the waiting `decision` (if any), the era kit, the place/income, the journal summary, and the allowed scene templates/dressings/sockets/characters.

Output: a validated `ScenePlan`:

```ts
interface ScenePlan {
  template: LifeTemplateId;       // e.g. "home_childhood", "school_classroom"
  dressing: string[];             // era/income/place variant ids
  title: string;
  summary: string;                // one sentence for the journal
  timeOfDay: "morning" | "noon" | "afternoon" | "evening" | "night";
  weather: "clear" | "rain" | "overcast" | "hot";
  cast: Array<{
    role: string;                 // mother, father, teacher, friend, partner, child, doctor
    name: string;
    age: number;
    characterId: string;          // existing GLB id
    mood: string;
    socket: string;
    lines: string[];
  }>;
  player: { socket: string; age: number; cameraHeight: number };
  dialogue: Array<{ speaker: string; text: string }>;
  interactables: Array<{
    id: string;
    socket: string;
    label: string;
    kind: "talk" | "look" | "use";
    choiceKey?: string;           // engine choice key
    focusKey?: string;            // engine focus key
  }>;
  choiceBindings: Record<string, string>;  // every engine choice key -> interactable id
  ambience: string;
}
```

Rules:

- The LLM may choose a template, dressing, cast, dialogue, and interaction bindings.
- The LLM may **not** invent life facts, change an outcome, add a choice the engine did not offer, or remove one.
- Every engine choice key must appear in `choiceBindings`, bound to an interactable that exists in the plan.
- Every `characterId` must be in the catalog; every `socket` must exist in the template; text lengths are capped.
- On validation failure, use the deterministic director’s plan for the same beat.
- Cache plans by `(decision.id | event key, state hash, era)` so a replay does not pay twice.
- Prompt injection guard: the engine’s event/decision text is data. The prompt wraps it in delimiters and tells the model to ignore instructions inside it.
- The prompt never contains the 1in8b key, the token, or the user’s LLM key.

**What gets staged (core rule, not the LLM).** The core decides which beats become scenes. A beat is stageable when it is one of: birth, first day of school, school stream or exam, first job, migration, partnership or marriage, first child, a parent’s death, a health crisis, retirement, death. Engine decisions in these domains are staged; other decisions are auto-resolved with the policy/default choice and logged. The cap is 16 scenes; if more major beats occur, rank by domain and keep the highest-stakes, summarizing the rest. The LLM may choose how to stage a selected beat, not whether the beat exists.

**Fact guard.** The scene director receives a compact fact sheet: every named person, their role, age, alive/dead status, the player’s job, partner, children, place, year, and the engine’s event/decision text. Validation rejects a plan that:

- names a person not in the fact sheet;
- gives a dead person a present-tense speaking line unless the scene is explicitly a memory or flashback;
- changes the player’s job, partner, children, place, or age;
- adds or removes an engine choice.

If the plan fails, the deterministic plan is used. The fact sheet is also the LLM’s only source of names and relationships; it must not invent a family member.

The deterministic director maps `decision.about` and event keywords to templates and writes short templated lines with the engine’s names/ages. It is the always-available path.

### 6.7 Scene system

Scenes are authored dioramas, not generated geometry. Each template is a small map (12–20m) with:

- floor, theme, lights, placeables (reusing the existing map schema);
- named sockets for the player and cast (`player_start`, `mother`, `father`, `teacher`, `friend`, `partner`, `bed`, `desk`, `window`, `altar`, `door`);
- interactable anchors;
- bounds for the first-person controller;
- a `life` metadata block: template id, allowed eras, incomes, places, and dressing ids.

Templates for v1:

| Template | Use |
| --- | --- |
| `birth_home` / `birth_hospital` | birth, first look |
| `home_childhood` | early childhood, family life |
| `home_family` | adult home, partner, children |
| `school_classroom` | first day, study, exams |
| `schoolyard` | friends, first love, conflict |
| `street_neighborhood` | city/village street, market, travel |
| `workplace_office` | office job |
| `workplace_shop` | shop, market stall, small business |
| `workplace_factory` | factory/industrial job |
| `farm_field` | rural work, rice field |
| `hospital` | illness, birth, deathbed |
| `temple_church` | faith, festival, funeral |
| `cemetery_memorial` | death, memorial |
| `wedding_home` | partnership, wedding |
| `station` | migration, leaving |

A scene can be reused across chapters with different dressing and cast. The director picks; the renderer resolves template + dressing + cast.

**Art approach (user decision).** Reuse the existing Kenney packs and add a small, hand-authored Vietnamese prop set — target 10–15 simple low-poly GLBs or primitive assemblies:

1. motorbike / scooter
2. bicycle
3. shophouse facade module (door, awning, balcony)
4. family altar with incense
5. plastic stool and folding table
6. market stall with produce baskets
7. woven rice basket and mat
8. conical hat (prop; no custom character model)
9. ceiling fan
10. era electronics (radio, CRT TV, cassette player, smartphone)
11. school desk and blackboard (if the existing furniture is not enough)
12. temple gate / cemetery incense burner

No custom character models in v1; wardrobes are color variants of the existing GLBs. Every new asset goes through `public/assets/ATTRIBUTION.md` and `npm run assets:inventory`.

### 6.8 Era kits

The player selects a period; the engine returns the exact year. The renderer maps the year to an era kit. The kit changes architecture, props, palette, signage, vehicles, clothing colors, and ambience — not the plot.

| Kit | Years | Home | Street | School/work | Signature props |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `war_subsidy` | 1950–1985 | thatch/brick, wooden furniture, altar | dirt lane, bicycles, low shops | wooden desks, chalkboard, state shop | radio, oil lamp, water jar, propaganda poster (careful), bicycle |
| `doi_moi` | 1986–1999 | brick/tile, tile floor, wooden sofa | motorbikes, shophouses, awnings | classroom, small office, market | motorbike, plastic stool, fan, cassette player, neon sign |
| `opening` | 2000–2015 | apartment/tube house, tiled floor | more traffic, cafés, karaoke | modern classroom, office | CRT/LCD, flip phone, air conditioner, coffee shop |
| `modern` | 2016–2026 | apartment/tube house, glass, plants | motorbike traffic, convenience stores | modern office, co-working | smartphone, laptop, scooter, air purifier |
| `future` | 2026+ (`future=1`) | climate-adapted home, green façade | EV traffic, heat shade, elevated rail | remote work, AI tools | solar shade, EV, AR glass, cooling fan |

v1 ships `doi_moi`, `opening`, `modern`, and `future`. The v1 period picker starts at 1986; `war_subsidy` (1950–1985) is the first expansion, not part of this build. This is the one visible scope limitation in the period picker.

### 6.9 Character casting and aging

- Cast roles from the engine state and events: mother, father, siblings, grandparents, teacher, classmates, friends, boss, coworkers, partner, children, doctor, neighbors.
- Use the existing 12 adult GLBs, with age scaling and wardrobe color variants. The rig already supports target height and head scale; the game passes an age-scaled height (child ~0.65–0.8, teen ~0.9, adult 1.0, elder ~0.95 with a slower idle).
- No custom character models in v1. If age scaling reads poorly, revisiting that is a later decision, not part of this build.
- The player is first-person; the body is visible in mirrors, windows, and the life review. No first-person hands in v1.
- NPCs are static or idle-animated at sockets; no wandering AI in scenes (the arena’s wander system stays in the arena).

### 6.10 Life facts vs. scene direction

This boundary is the core design rule:

- **Engine:** who you are, where and when you were born, your family, your health, your schooling, your jobs, your partner, your children, your moves, your death.
- **LLM:** the room, the light, who is standing where, what they say, which object holds which choice.
- **Deterministic fallback:** a template + cast + short lines, so the game never blocks on the LLM.
- **Player:** the engine’s choices, expressed as physical interactions.

If the LLM’s scene contradicts the engine, the engine wins and the plan is rejected.

## 7. Data and persistence

- `agent-arena-life`: current run, journal, thoughts, chapter/scene index, completed lives.
- `agent-arena-life-key`: the 1in8b key, separate and clearable.
- Reuse the app’s existing model config for the scene director; do not duplicate LLM keys.
- Completed lives keep: engine version, config, token (until expiry), name, years, cause of death, journal, thoughts, scene stills if cheap. The token is not needed after death; keep the summary.
- A “clear my lives” action deletes the run and completed lives.
- No analytics, no server, no upload.

## 8. LLM usage and cost

- One scene-director call per staged scene (12–16 per run), plus retries.
- Use the app’s selected model; prefer a cheap, fast model for scene direction. A “template scenes only” option skips LLM calls entirely.
- The prompt contains: the scene schema, the allowed templates/sockets/characters, the engine state and events, the decision, and the era/place notes. It does not contain the 1in8b key, token, or the user’s LLM key.
- Output is JSON only. Validate; on failure, retry once at temperature 0, then fall back.
- Cache by beat + state hash + era. A replay of the same beat reuses the plan.
- Cap output tokens (e.g., 900) and truncate cast/dialogue to keep latency low.
- If the LLM is slow, show the deterministic scene immediately and swap in the LLM version only if it arrives before the player acts (optional; simpler to wait with a short transition).

## 9. Safety and content

- Ages 15+. The start screen states that the game contains death, illness, and hardship, and that every life is simulated.
- `sensitive: 0`, `suicide: 0` by default. The game does not request the hardest lines and does not depict drug dependence, sexual violence, domestic violence, self-harm, or suicide.
- War and historical violence are shown through radio, newspapers, absence, and aftermath — never combat, never graphic imagery.
- Death is off-screen and quiet. No medical detail. The life review focuses on the life, not the death.
- No gambling, betting, or real-money mechanics.
- No ranking of people by faith, ethnicity, caste, gender, orientation, or disability. Difference is shown as the data shows it, never as a hierarchy.
- A footer link to support resources in the pause/settings, worded gently.
- The engine credit and “not a real person” line appear on the birth card, journal, death review, and memorial.
- The LLM prompt forbids graphic content and instructs the model to keep hard events off-screen.

## 10. Testing and verification

### Unit

- `oneIn8b` request building, error mapping, token handling, retry rules, usage parsing.
- `sceneDirector` schema validation, choice-binding completeness, fallback on invalid output, prompt-injection delimiters.
- `deterministicDirector` mapping for every major decision domain and key event.
- Era mapping: year → kit, future flag, boundaries.
- Chapter planning: scene cap, early death, migration, no partner/children.
- Journal ordering, summaries, thoughts.
- Storage: run persistence, key separation, corrupt data, expired token.

### Contract

- A mocked `/live` sequence covering birth → decision → focus → timeskip → death, with the real response shapes.
- A small internal fixture set for adapter tests. Do not publish engine answers as a dataset; keep fixtures minimal and out of the shipped bundle.

### Manual

- Full playthroughs: `doi_moi`, `opening`, `modern`, `future`.
- Early death (infant/child), no marriage, no children, migration, disability, rural/urban, low/high income.
- No LLM key (template path) and a failing LLM (fallback path).
- Invalid/expired 1in8b key, quota exhausted, rate limit, offline.
- Keyboard-only, touch, reduced motion, subtitles.
- Performance: 60fps target on a mid-range laptop; scene load under 3s; no leak across 16 scenes.

### Repo gates

`npm run lint`, `npm test`, `npm run build` must pass. New logic under `src/game` gets tests even though the coverage threshold is scoped to `src/lib`.

## 11. Build phases

| Phase | Deliverable | Proves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Engine proof | Setup screen, `/live` client, text-only life debug: start, step, choose, reach death | The contract, units, error handling |
| 1. One scene | First-person scaffold, birth/home scene, one character, one decision, journal, birth card | The feel of being inside a beat |
| 2. Full arc | Chapter director, 8–10 templates, deterministic director, time-skips, death/memorial, save/resume, one era kit (`modern`) | A complete birth-to-death run |
| 3. Eras | `doi_moi`, `opening`, period selector, era dressing, age-scaling polish | The historical sweep |
| 4. LLM + polish | Scene director prompt and validation, dialogue, audio, accessibility, performance, tests | The game feels directed, not templated |
| 5. Future and beyond | `future` kit, cohort of same-second lives, more countries | Expansion |

The user’s “whole arc” requirement is satisfied by phase 2, before the LLM and era polish. That ordering keeps the risk on the arc, not the assets.

## 12. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Asset scope explodes | Authored dioramas, modular dressing, reuse Kenney packs; 3 eras + future in v1 |
| The LLM invents facts or breaks choices | Strict schema, choice-binding validation, engine-wins rule, deterministic fallback |
| API key leaks | BYO key, separate storage, never in prompts/logs/run JSON, no analytics |
| Quota/rate limits ruin a run | Show usage, cache plans, chunk steps, graceful quota screen, token resume |
| A life ends early or takes an unexpected path | Chapter plan is a target; the director adapts; early death is a valid full arc |
| The 3D feels like a text game with a backdrop | Choices are physical interactions; the journal holds the text; scenes have local detail and people |
| Sensitive content mishandled | Engine defaults off, prompt guardrails, off-screen death/war, review pass |
| Performance on low-end machines | Lazy-load, one scene at a time, dispose assets, quality settings, 30fps floor |
| The period selector promises more than v1 ships | Clearly label the supported range; 1950–1985 marked as later |

## 13. Resolved decisions and open calls

The four questions that changed what gets built are settled:

- **Art:** kitbash from the existing Kenney packs plus 10–15 hand-authored Vietnamese props; no custom character models in v1.
- **Early era:** 1950–1985 is a later expansion; v1 starts at 1986.
- **Language:** English UI and dialogue first, with Vietnamese names and terms; Vietnamese localization later.
- **Key:** the player brings their own `k8b_` key; the app stays client-only, and the engine client is built behind a transport interface so a proxy can be added later.

The remaining calls are for the builder, with defaults:

1. **Session length target.** Default: 45–75 minutes, 12–16 scenes.
2. **Cohort of babies born in the same second.** Default: defer; the `/live` API does not return it directly.
3. **Exact title and naming.** *Một Đời* is a working title; the final name must not imply endorsement by 1in8b.
4. **Audio.** Default: ambient in phase 4; music optional.
5. **Death review form.** Default: stills + journal + thoughts; a 3D memorial hall is a later expansion.
